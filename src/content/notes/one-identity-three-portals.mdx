A role-based application needs one answer to "who is this user?" and several
answers to "what can this user do?" Mixing those questions produces large view
modules filled with role checks. Separating them creates a clearer boundary.

## Centralize identity

The college portal extends Django's user model with a role and creates the
matching HOD, staff, or student profile. Email authentication and account
identity stay in one place. The role determines which portal the user enters
after sign-in.

That avoids maintaining unrelated authentication systems for users who still
belong to the same institution.

## Separate operational workflows

The three roles interact with the same academic domain but own different
actions:

- HOD users manage courses, sessions, staff, students, approvals, and replies.
- Staff users record attendance, enter results, and submit leave or feedback.
- Students review records and submit their own requests.

Dedicated view modules keep those request handlers apart while the relational
model connects subjects, sessions, attendance, results, leave, and feedback.

## The production caveat

Role routing is not sufficient authorization by itself. Every sensitive view
still needs server-side permission checks, and production records should not be
distributed through a committed SQLite database or uploaded-media folder. Test
fixtures, migrations, external storage, and privacy-aware seed data are safer
boundaries.

The broader lesson is that shared identity and separated workflows complement
each other. One login system simplifies account management; explicit portal
boundaries make authorization and maintenance easier to audit.
